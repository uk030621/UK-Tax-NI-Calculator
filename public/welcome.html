One worked example — the High Income Child Benefit Charge

A household earning £76,000, receiving £2,212.60 of Child Benefit over the year, owes an extra

£1,770.08

— 80% of the £2,212.60 received, clawed back because £76,000 is 80% of the way through the £60,000–£80,000 taper.

A second worked example — higher-rate pension relief (including AVCs)

A higher-rate taxpayer earning £80,000 who pays £8,000 into a relief-at-source pension or AVC scheme (£10,000 once the 20% their provider already added is included) is owed a further

£2,000

— the difference between the 40% relief they're entitled to and the 20% their provider already applied automatically. Our calculator works this out for you — look for "Additional pension relief" in your results, with no manual grossing-up required.

How to actually get this money back

  1. Check your scheme type. This only applies to relief-at-source contributions. Net pay or salary sacrifice contributions already give you full relief automatically — nothing further to claim.
  2. Use the figure our calculator gives you — no need to work out the grossing-up by hand.
  3. Submit it to HMRC — through your Self Assessment return, or via your online HMRC Personal Tax Account if you don't normally file one.
  4. Check previous years too. If you've never claimed before, you can usually backdate unclaimed relief up to 4 previous tax years.

No — it's an independent calculator built to produce an accurate estimate, not a Self Assessment submission tool. That's stated clearly throughout, including on every page of results.

What happens to my data?

Your figures calculate your result and, if you choose to save it, are stored against your account so you can come back to it later. Nothing is sold or shared.

What if my situation isn't covered?

The app lists every known gap honestly, with where to get real advice for each one — a Chartered Accountant, a Chartered Tax Adviser, or specific HMRC guidance, depending on what's missing.

Yes — your one-off payment covers every tax year the calculator supports, for as long as it exists, not a single calculation. This is maintained by one person, not a team, so there's no fixed release date — but keeping it genuinely current each year is the whole point of the product.
